## 0. Corrections to the project plan's background (please read first)

1. **A nationwide class was certified on Oct 8, 2026.** The case is *Freestyle World, Inc. v. CBP*, Ct. No. 26-01088, before Judge Eaton.
   - The class is "all importers of record who paid duties imposed by the Challenged Tariff Orders" between Feb 1, 2025 and Feb 19, 2026. Importers that filed their own suits are excluded.
   - It was certified under Rule 23(b)(2), so there is no opt-out.
   - DWT says it "presumably covers" non-plaintiffs' finally liquidated entries. **No classwide relief order has been issued yet.**
   - IEEPA duties on Brazilian and Indian goods, and duties collected after Feb 19, are not covered. [S: DWT, Oct 14, 2026]
   - Diaz Trade Law (Oct 19) reports that the class does not unlock CAPE Phase 3, and that the government had not yet appealed the certification. [S]

   So "finally liquidated = plaintiffs only" is still true *for CAPE Phase 3 today*. But the app should say "class member; relief pending" instead of "no route."
2. **A second deadline matters for finally liquidated entries.** The window to file your own CIT suit under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i) runs "through February 4, 2027, at the earliest" (two years from payment). [S: DWT] The CIT filing fee for such an action is **$400**. [P: CIT Schedule of Fees]
3. **The House letter says "90 days," not 80.** Rep. Stevens and nine others wrote on Sept 9, 2026 of a "90-day deadline." CBP's own page says Phase 1 covers entries "liquidated within the preceding 80 days." [P: both] Use CBP's 80.
4. **The government's appeal** of the universal refund order was filed June 3, 2026. One secondary source gives the docket as Fed. Cir. No. 26-1895 (not verified). DOJ's opening brief was reportedly filed Aug 10, 2026. As of Oct 19, 2026 the appeal is pending with no ruling. [S: Diaz Trade Law, Foley]

## 6. Interest on refunds

- **Legal basis:**
  - Interest is owed under 19 U.S.C. 1505. Under **19 CFR 24.36** ("Refunds of excessive duties, taxes, etc."), it runs from the date of deposit to the date of liquidation or reliquidation, at the rate set under 19 CFR 24.3a(c)(1).
  - **No interest accrues** when CBP certifies an electronic refund but cannot pay it because the importer gave no bank details.
  - [S: Cornell LII text of 19 CFR 24.36, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/24.36]
  - CBP says interest is included in the refund and shown separately on ACE report ES-022. [P: CBP IEEPA refunds page, last modified Sept 30, 2026, https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds]
- **Overpayment rates** [P: CBP notice FR Doc. 2026-19292, published Sept 22, 2026, https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-19292.pdf]:

| Period | Non-corporate | Corporate |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 1, 2025 – Mar 31, 2026 | 7% | 6% |
| Apr 1 – Jun 30, 2026 | 6% | 5% |
| Jul 1 – Dec 31, 2026 | **7%** | **6%** |

  (The Q4 2026 notice says the rates "will remain the same." A later paragraph says they "will increase." The table itself is unambiguous.)
- **Monthly dollar figure:**
  - The CIT said "approximately **$650 million** accruing per month," and "$10 billion" by year-end if entries were not liquidated. [P: *Atmus* order, Mar 6, 2026; that was on about $165B outstanding] The Chamber repeats the $650M. [P]
  - **No current official monthly figure.** [NF]
  - A rough current estimate: about $30–40B still outstanding × 6% ÷ 12 ≈ **$150–200M per month**. [D]

## 8. Savings model: one illustrative small importer

**Everything in this section is illustrative. Items marked A# are assumptions, not data.**

### Setup

- **A1.** The importer is a corporation. It paid **$50,000 in IEEPA duties on 12 formal entries**, one on the 15th of each month from Mar 2025 to Feb 2026. That is about $4,167 per entry, each with an IEEPA line.
- **A2.** Each entry liquidates **314 days after entry**. That is ACE's default auto-liquidation [P: Lord decl. ¶6], with no extension or suspension.
- **A3.** Nothing has been filed yet: no CAPE, no protest, no lawsuit. There are no exclusions (surety, type 08, drawback, AD/CVD).
- **A4.** Today is Oct 24, 2026. Routing follows the project's rules table: CAPE if liquidation was 80 days ago or less, or if unliquidated; protest if liquidation was 81–180 days ago; finally liquidated if more than 180 days ago.
- **A5.** Interest is estimated at **6% simple** (the corporate rate) from entry to an assumed reliquidation 60 days from now. Actual interest compounds daily and the rate was 5% in Q2 2026, so this is only a rough figure.

### Entry routing (computed)

| Bucket | Entries (entry month) | Principal | Est. interest (A5) | Nearest deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| File in CAPE now | Oct 2025 (CAPE deadline **Nov 13**, 20 days), Nov 2025 (Dec 14), Dec 2025, Jan 2026, Feb 2026 (still unliquidated) | **$20,831** | ~$1,275 | Nov 13, 2026 |
| File a protest | Jul 2025 (deadline **Nov 21**, 28 days), Aug 2025 (Dec 22), Sep 2025 (Jan 22, 2027) | **$12,501** | ~$1,017 | Nov 21, 2026 |
| Finally liquidated | Mar–Jun 2025 | **$16,668** | ~$1,650 | No CAPE route. Class member (relief pending), or own CIT suit by about Feb 4, 2027 at the earliest |
| **Recoverable now** | 8 entries | **$33,332** | **~$2,292** | **≈ $35,624** |

### Scenarios (from today)

| Scenario | Fees and costs | Net recovered now | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. No action** | $0 | **$0** | Every month, more entries slide from CAPE to protest to finally liquidated. Within about 3 months all $50,000 sits in the "class/appeal only" bucket, and its outcome is unknown. **No probability is assigned.** |
| **B1. Broker, flat per entry** | **A6:** $200–$350 × 8 entries = $1,600–$2,800 (rate from a vendor blog, [S, weak]) | **$32,824–$34,024** | Depends on the broker's queue. Missing the Nov 13 or Nov 21 deadlines would cost about $4.2k each. |
| **B2. Contingency firm, 5–15%** | **A7:** 5–15% × $35,624 = $1,781–$5,344 | **$30,280–$33,843** | Advertised ranges, [S, weak] |
| **B3. Sell the claim at 70–90%** | gives up 10–30% = $3,562–$10,687 | **$24,937–$32,062** | Liberty Justice Center range. Selling does not speed up CBP. |
| **B4. 20–35% (class-counsel norm; high-end reference)** | $7,125–$12,468 | $23,156–$28,499 | Not a quoted price for CAPE work. Use only as a ceiling. |
| **C. Self-file with a triage tool, broker only where needed** | **A8:** tool $0 (student demo). **A9:** broker files the 3 protests and the 2 urgent liquidated CAPE entries, because ACE approval can take 2–8 weeks; 5 × $200–$350 = $1,000–$1,750. **A10:** owner time 3–6 hours × $50/hour = $150–$300. | **$33,574–$34,474** | Self-files the 3 unliquidated entries once ACE is approved. CBP charges $0. |

### What the model shows

1. **The money that matters is the deadline money, not the fee money.** Acting instead of doing nothing is worth about **$35.6k** of this importer's $50k. Self-filing instead of paying a contingency firm saves only about **$0.5k–$4k** (C vs B2) [D].
2. **The cost of a 30-day delay** (acting Nov 23 instead of today):
   - The Jul 2025 entry passes its Nov 21 protest deadline and becomes finally liquidated: **about $4,167 plus about $360 interest moves to "no current route."**
   - The Oct 2025 entry passes Nov 13 and moves from CAPE to protest. It is still recoverable, but now needs a protest.
   - [D]

   This is the "money at risk this week" story.
3. **Finally liquidated money ($16.7k here):** Using A6 and the $10–15k attorney quotes, a solo CIT suit would cost about **$10.4k–$15.4k** ($400 fee plus attorney), for $16.7k plus interest at stake. Staying in the *Freestyle World* class is the low-cost option, which matches Diaz Trade Law's advice for small claims. [D]
4. **Sensitivity:** If the importer had filed nothing since April, it would already have lost CAPE eligibility on the 4 oldest entries. The model assumes they missed their protest windows too, which is the A3 premise.
